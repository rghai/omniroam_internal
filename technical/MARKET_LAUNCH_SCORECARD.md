# Launch market scorecard

Checked 31 August 2026.

## Decision

Australia is a reasonable first acquisition test. The raw data do not prove that it is the largest or objectively best source market.

The product should remain global. The Australian version should be one market package, not the whole company identity. The recommended structure is `/au/en` for the Australian campaign and `/global/en` for neutral English. Add other path-based versions after measured demand. Do not use country subdomains yet.

IP location may suggest a market but must never force a redirect. Save the customer-selected market and language on the order. Use that saved choice for checkout, legal copy, customer support routing and transactional email.

## What the evidence supports

Australia is the smallest market in this comparison by raw outbound volume. It still offers a practical test because:

- 12.7 million annual short trips are enough to learn from a focused campaign.
- 60.8 per cent of Australian trips are holidays and the median trip is 14 days.
- Indonesia, Japan, Thailand and Vietnam combine high Australian traffic with supplier packages that have workable wholesale costs.
- Australian English and AUD let the founders judge the offer, legal conditions and support copy directly.
- Telstra, Optus and Vodafone create a visible daily-roaming price reference.

These are execution advantages. They are not proof that Australian lifetime value or acquisition cost is better.

## Test that would close the decision

Run an Australia campaign, a neutral global-English control and one second English-market test. Set the thresholds before buying traffic:

- visit-to-checkout conversion;
- checkout-to-paid conversion;
- customer acquisition cost;
- gross margin after payment FX, supplier cost, support and refunds;
- successful installation rate;
- support contacts per order;
- refund and chargeback rate.

Australia remains the priority only if the measured result clears the founder-approved thresholds. Otherwise, change the market rather than defending the original choice.
