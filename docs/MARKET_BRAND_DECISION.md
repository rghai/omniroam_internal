# Market and brand decision

Date: 2026-08-29

## Decision

Use Australia as Omniroam's first acquisition market. Focus the first 90 days on Australian leisure travellers taking roughly 7–21 day trips to Indonesia/Bali, Japan, Thailand, Vietnam, and multi-country Europe. Keep the sellable catalogue and underlying product globally extensible.

Australia is a launch wedge, not a permanent geographic limit. The decision must be reconsidered after 100 paid Australian orders or 90 days, whichever comes first.

Keep **Omniroam** as the working consumer name. Use **“Land connected.”** as the core promise and **Omi** as a friendly signal-sprite mascot. The working visual system uses cream, coral, mint, sunny yellow, and dark ink. Retain rename-ready configuration until trademark, domain, handle, and language checks are complete.

## Why Australia: the numerical case

Australia is not the world's largest outbound market. It is the best first test because it combines enough volume, a high share of leisure travel, unusually clean destination fit, meaningful carrier-roaming price pain, and a payment setup that is already close to usable.

### 1. There is enough demand for a focused first market

The Australian Bureau of Statistics recorded **12,711,650 short-term resident returns in 2025–26**, up about 4% in one year and 32% over ten years. Holidays represented **60.8%** of trips, implying roughly **7.73 million holiday trips**. The median overseas trip lasted **14 days**, and the median holiday lasted **13 days**.

This is far larger than Omniroam needs to validate an early product. Capturing 100 paid orders would represent about **0.0013%** of one year's estimated Australian holiday trips.

### 2. The first catalogue matches where Australians actually travel

Four of the initial Asian destinations accounted for **3,961,560 trips** in 2025–26:

| Destination | Australian trips | Share of all outbound trips |
|---|---:|---:|
| Indonesia | 1,777,270 | 14.0% |
| Japan | 992,550 | 7.8% |
| Thailand | 624,260 | 4.9% |
| Vietnam | 567,480 | 4.5% |
| **Four-destination total** | **3,961,560** | **31.2%** |

Japan travel was **105.2% above 2018–19 volume**, and Vietnam was **76.0% above 2018–19**. These are not arbitrary launch tiles; they map directly to large, growing travel flows. Multi-country Europe gives the catalogue an additional long-haul use case.

### 3. The saving can be large enough to explain in one sentence

Current Australian carrier offers provide a clear price anchor:

- Telstra lists **A$10/day** for its main international day-pass zones, including 2GB/day plus calls and SMS.
- Optus advertises eligible **A$5/day** roaming with 5GB/day.
- Vodafone advertises **A$5/day** roaming on eligible plans in more than 100 destinations.

For a 13-day holiday, that is an anchor of **A$65–A$130** when the pass is used every day. Omniroam's working 5–10GB launch prices of **A$10.99–A$17.99** create a potential data-cost saving of roughly **A$47–A$119**, or **72%–92%**, versus those daily-pass anchors.

This comparison must remain honest: Omniroam is data-only and does not include the customer's usual mobile number, carrier voice/SMS inclusions, or the daily data volumes of every carrier pass.

### 4. The first operational setup favours an AUD launch

The existing Opn Thailand account can support AUD presentment when multi-currency is enabled. Opn documents card-only Visa/Mastercard support and an approximate **2%–4% FX spread** for multi-currency charges. This is not perfect economics, but it reduces the number of new systems required for the first test. The effect of settlement currency, fees, tax, refunds, and support cost must be measured in actual orders.

## Alternative launch markets

These markets are credible alternatives, not rejected ideas. Australia wins the first test on combined fit and execution simplicity; the others remain explicit expansion hypotheses.

| Market | Quantitative signal | Strength | Why it is not first | Recommended next move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Singapore** | 10.67m resident departures in 2025, including 8.85m by air | High travel frequency, English-first, strong Asia proximity | Smaller absolute customer base and likely stronger familiarity with regional roaming/eSIM options | Best small-market backup test after Australia; localise SGD pricing and compare carrier bundles first |
| **United Kingdom** | 94.6m overseas visits and £78.6bn spend in 2024; Spain, France, and Italy alone received 31.9m visits | Very large, Europe-heavy demand that fits regional plans | Requires GBP commercial setup, UK-specific consumer/tax review, and a Europe-led proposition; official 2024 figures are still labelled “in development” | Phase-two Europe proposition after Australian conversion and support economics are known |
| **United States** | 107.7m outbound travellers in 2024; 53.8m went overseas and 61% of those trips were holidays | Largest scale option, long average overseas stay of 15.3 nights, high reported traveller income | 54.0m trips went to Mexico or Canada, where incumbent-plan inclusion can weaken the eSIM pitch; launch competition and paid-acquisition complexity are much higher | Enter by destination or traveller segment, not as a broad national launch |
| **Canada** | 42.9m visits abroad in 2024; separate border data recorded 13.0m overseas trips | English/French affluent travel market; overseas trips exceeded 2019 levels | Most outbound volume is US travel:30.2m of the 42.9m visits in the national survey:so the total overstates the relevant eSIM pool | Test an overseas-only proposition after the UK/US catalogue and support playbook exist |

### Expansion priority

1. **Australia:** launch and measure.
2. **Singapore:** smallest operationally adjacent alternative and fastest controlled comparison.
3. **United Kingdom:** strongest Europe-led scale market.
4. **United States:** largest upside, but only with segment-specific acquisition.
5. **Canada:** attractive overseas niche, but not the headline 42.9m total.

## Launch pricing hypothesis

Prices are AUD and remain subject to tax, actual Opn settlement, support/refund cost, and competitor revalidation.

| Product | Wholesale USD | Launch AUD |
|---|---:|---:|
| Japan/Indonesia/Thailand 1GB, 7d | 0.70 | 4.99 |
| Japan/Indonesia/Thailand 5GB, 30d | 2.70 | 10.99 |
| Japan/Indonesia/Thailand 10GB, 30d | 4.70 | 15.99 |
| Vietnam 5GB, 30d | 3.77 | 12.99 |
| Europe 33 areas 5GB, 30d | 2.82 | 11.99 |
| Europe 33 areas 10GB, 30d | 5.86 | 17.99 |

Hard guardrails: minimum **A$3 contribution** and **35% post-payment contribution margin**. AI may recommend pricing but may not lower these floors.

## What would change the decision

Australia should lose first-market status if the initial evidence shows any of the following:

- fewer than 2% of qualified product-page sessions start checkout after two materially different offers;
- paid acquisition cannot recover its cost within the agreed contribution-margin window;
- payment acceptance, FX, refund, or tax friction makes the AUD setup structurally uneconomic;
- support contacts exceed 15 per 100 fulfilled orders after onboarding improvements;
- Singapore or UK tests produce at least 30% better contribution per qualified session on comparable traffic.
