Operating manual

Automate the routine. Put evidence in front of humans for sensitive decisions.

The goal is low oversight when healthy:not invisible risk. Alerts should be actionable, and every financial exception should be auditable.

On this page Customer support Marketing & growth IT & engineering Finance & revenue Runbooks

1 · Customer Support

Tier 1 · automated

Compatibility, order status, approved setup steps, magic-link requests and rate-limited resends. Answers come only from versioned material.

Tier 2 · automated investigation

Authenticated payment/provider lookups, diagnostics and safe idempotent reconciliation. It cannot refund, create another order or invent policy.

Tier 3 · human

Complaints, ambiguous money, novel technical cases, vendor escalation, goodwill and every refund approval.

Support cockpit

One verified timeline for order, payment, profile, diagnostics, customer contact, policy and recommended next action.

2 · Marketing and Growth

  • Founders edit approved content, offers, semantic colours and simple layout choices through previewable configuration.
  • Low-risk copy, metadata and broken-link fixes can be automated.
  • Landing-page variants, global design changes, pricing, legal/payment copy and bulk messages need founder review.
  • Track discovery, plan selection, checkout, paid orders, fulfilment, support and refunds through a vendor-neutral event model.
  • Service messages and marketing consent stay separate; marketing is unchecked by default.
  • Promotions cannot breach approved margin floors.

3 · IT / Engineering Operations

Healthy systems should be quiet. Alert only when someone can act: payment uncertainty, paid-but-unfulfilled orders, webhook backlog, provider failure, stale catalog, margin breach, email failure or reconciliation mismatch.

Required controls

  • Structured logs and correlation IDs across checkout, payment and supplier calls.
  • Health metrics, service targets, bounded retries and visible dead letters.
  • Separate preview/live secrets, write enablement and emergency kill switches.
  • Checkpointed scheduled reconciliation jobs.
  • Database backups, restore tests and replay-safe recovery.

4 · Financial / Revenue Operations

THB is the financial home currency.

The customer sale is recorded in AUD, Opn’s actual funding amount and net settlement are recorded in THB, and eSIMAccess cost is recorded in USD. Keep every original amount and rate; do not reconstruct history from today’s FX.

  • Every paid order line keeps immutable AUD retail, THB gross/net settlement, USD wholesale cost, fees, VAT, FX rates and realised THB margin snapshots.
  • Daily automation matches internal orders to Opn charges/refunds/fees and supplier orders/costs.
  • Contribution in THB starts with actual Opn net THB received minus the actual THB cost of funding USD supplier spend, then tax, support, refunds and other variable costs.
  • Mismatch, missing records, abnormal fees, FX variance and margin breaches enter an exception queue.
  • Automation may calculate a refund recommendation; a human approves amount, reason and eligibility.
  • Refund proposal, approval, execution and verification are separate audited steps.
  • Gateway and treasury optimisation is a measured later workstream: compare all-in FX, card fees, approval rates, payout timing, refunds and operational burden.
  • No agent may lower margin floors, approve its own refund or silently write off a mismatch.

Incident runbooks

Before launch, turn each into a one-page checklist and drill it in test mode:

  1. Payment outcome unknown
  2. Paid order not provisioned
  3. Duplicate callback or duplicate-risk event
  4. Supplier outage or slow allocation
  5. Stale usage data
  6. Catalog or supplier-cost spike
  7. Margin-floor breach
  8. Email delivery failure
  9. Refund execution ambiguous
  10. Credential rotation and suspected exposure
