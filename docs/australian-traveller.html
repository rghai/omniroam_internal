Market research

What Australians dislike about roaming

A directional review of community discussion, consumer advice and current comparison material, checked 29 August 2026.

Executive finding

Price matters, but the market is won by reducing arrival stress without hiding product limits. Travellers want data for maps, rides and hotel details as soon as they land. They also worry about keeping their Australian number, bank verification SMS, setup mistakes and “unlimited” plans that slow down.

Recommended hierarchy

Lead with calm arrival and setup before departure. Use the clear AUD total as proof. Follow with honest data-only, carrier-SMS and throttling explanations.

Repeated pain points

  1. Trip-total shock. A daily pass is easy to understand but expensive when multiplied across two or three weeks.
  2. Airport dependency. Maps, rides, messages and hotel details are most useful immediately after landing.
  3. Keeping the home number. Australian bank and account verification SMS creates anxiety. The common pattern is to leave the Australian SIM active and use the travel eSIM for data.
  4. Setup uncertainty. People prefer to install and troubleshoot at home on reliable Wi-Fi.
  5. Data-only confusion. Calls, SMS and a local number are not assumed consistently.
  6. Unlimited fine print. Fair-use throttling and hotspot restrictions damage trust.
  7. Local SIM trade-off. Local prepaid can be cheaper for a long stay, while a travel eSIM is valued for instant and regional coverage.
  8. Foreign checkout costs. AUD helps customers understand the final amount and avoid surprise bank fees.

Copy and product rules

  • Say “install before you fly”, not only “ready in minutes”.
  • Explain how to keep the Australian SIM in place without promising overseas SMS availability.
  • Show data-only before payment.
  • Admit when a local SIM may suit a long stay better.
  • Never say unlimited without the full-speed allowance, fair-use speed and hotspot rules.
  • Build regional plans around multi-country convenience.
  • Put exact carrier claims in a dated register and remove them automatically after expiry.
