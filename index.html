Market decision

Why Australia wins the first test

It is a measured launch wedge—not a feeling and not a forever decision.

Australia is large enough to validate the product, highly leisure-led, closely aligned with our cheapest and strongest Asian catalogue, and gives us a clear saving story against A$5–A$10 daily roaming. We review the choice after 100 paid orders or 90 days.

12.71mAustralian short-term resident trips in 2025–26
7.73mEstimated holiday trips, based on the 60.8% holiday share
3.96mTrips to Indonesia, Japan, Thailand, and Vietnam alone
13 daysMedian Australian holiday—A$65–A$130 at A$5–A$10/day

At working prices of A$10.99–A$17.99, a data-only Omniroam plan could save roughly A$47–A$119 against those daily-pass anchors. The comparison is not like-for-like: carrier passes may include the customer's usual number, calls, SMS, and more daily data. Read the calculations and official sources →

Fastest backup test

Singapore

10.67m resident departures in 2025, including 8.85m by air. Strong travel frequency, English-first operations, and Asia proximity; smaller scale and likely tougher incumbent offers make it a controlled comparison rather than the first bet.

Best Europe expansion

United Kingdom

94.6m overseas visits and £78.6bn spend in 2024. Huge Europe-led demand fits regional plans, but it adds GBP, UK consumer/tax work, and a new acquisition battlefield before we know baseline unit economics.

Largest long-term upside

United States

107.7m outbound travellers in 2024, including 53.8m overseas. It is enormous but not a clean broad launch: Mexico and Canada alone account for 54.0m trips, and national customer acquisition is much more complex.

Who we sell to and how we show up

Market and brand

Start with Australian leisure travellers taking roughly 7–21 day trips. Lead with a friendly, low-stress promise and transparent prices—not telecom jargon.

Explore the important decisions
  • Australia has enough holiday volume and unusually strong initial-destination fit.
  • Use a 100-order or 90-day evidence gate.
  • Keep Omniroam and “Land connected.” as the working brand promise.
  • Keep Singapore, the UK, the US, and Canada as explicit expansion hypotheses.

How we avoid expensive mistakes

MVP architecture

The storefront, checkout, supplier connection, payments, and founder tools begin as one manageable application with replaceable provider adapters.

Explore the important decisions
  • One customer payment can create no more than one supplier order.
  • The review build uses test payments and demo fulfilment by default.
  • Live fulfilment stays off until durable controls and recovery tools exist.
  • Vercel plus managed Postgres is the intended production shape.

How two founders avoid babysitting it

Operations model

Routine support, monitoring, reconciliation, and reporting should happen automatically inside explicit limits. Humans retain sensitive decisions.

Explore the important decisions
  • Automate Support Tiers 1–2; route sensitive or unresolved cases to human Tier 3.
  • Give founders previewable controls for content and simple design changes.
  • Alert founders only for actionable exceptions with evidence.
  • Automate reconciliation but require human approval for every refund.

What can change without a rebuild

Configuration

Brand, launch market, destinations, prices, providers, and safety switches live in clear configuration. Credentials remain outside the code.

Explore the important decisions
  • Brand and commercial defaults stay separate from integration code.
  • Demo, test-payment, and supplier modes use explicit switches.
  • Supplier writes are disabled separately and capped for experiments.
  • Credentials shared during development must be rotated before production.
